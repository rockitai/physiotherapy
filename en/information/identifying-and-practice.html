How to Identify & Exercise

01How to identify the pelvic floor muscles

When the pelvic floor muscles contract, it feels like "closing" the openings (the urethra, the vaginal opening and the anus), together with a feeling of "lifting" the openings away from the surface we are sitting on.

The pelvic floor muscles cannot be seen, but there are several ways to identify their action:

  1. 1.Looking in a small mirror to see the perineum (the area between the vaginal opening and the anus) "draw in" when the pelvic floor muscles contract.
  2. 2.The "stop test" (I stress that this is only a test, and it must not be used as a regular way of exercising the pelvic floor muscles, because contracting while urinating as a routine exercise can harm the bladder): while urinating, try to stop the flow at its strongest for 2-3 seconds. Then finish urinating, but remember the action.

02Why I don't believe in "Just squeeze your pelvic floor…"

The pelvic floor is part of our breathing and postural muscles, and as such it always works in coordination with the breath and with the abdominal muscles. In my view, exercise that deals only with pelvic floor contractions and ignores the importance of the abdominal muscles and the breath is incomplete, and does not give a proper, full solution to most pelvic floor problems.

In addition, when there is excess tension in the pelvic floor (for example pain, or difficulty emptying), "squeezing" exercises can even make the problem worse.

So the right and best way is to be examined by a professional, get a thorough assessment of the state of your pelvic floor and abdominal muscles, and make sure the exercises are tailored personally to you.

03So how is it best to practice on your own?

  1. 01

    If you still choose to "try it yourself", the way to make sure the pelvic floor is recruited more correctly is to start the action with an exhale (= breathing out), without first taking in more air, while lowering the rib cage (narrowing it), and then to "accompany" the exhale with a contraction of the pelvic floor muscles.

  2. 02

    Another way to practice, in addition to the first method (and not instead of it), is to try a quick exhale (as in a cough, for example) and then accompany the cough or the quick exhale with a contraction of the pelvic floor muscles. This exercise trains the muscle to respond quickly, because besides the muscle's general strength, we want it to be skilled enough to engage quickly "to protect us.

In fact we can (and even should) accompany our everyday actions (especially those that involve effort) with an exhale together with a pelvic floor contraction – this way we give better protection to our spine and to our abdominal and pelvic organs.
