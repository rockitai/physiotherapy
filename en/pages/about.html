About

Nice to meet you, I'm Sivan Navot.

I have been a physiotherapist since 2000, and the pelvic floor has been my specialty almost from the start. I treat women and men, from childbearing age to after menopause, at my clinic in Herzliya and at the clinic in Ramat HaChayal, Tel Aviv.

As a pelvic floor physiotherapist I work with the whole range of problems related to bladder and bowel control and emptying, pain in the genitals and the groin, pelvic organ prolapse (uterine, bladder or rectal prolapse), constipation, hemorrhoids, back pain, pain in the pelvic and hip joints, and more.

Pelvic floor problems can seriously affect quality of life: social, professional and sexual function, and self-image. Although the condition is common, there is still a taboo around it, and many of those affected do not seek treatment out of embarrassment or because they do not know about the options for diagnosis and treatment. I invite you to get past the barrier of shame, read through the site and get in touch.

Wishing you complete health,
Sivan

My professional path

I completed my physiotherapy studies at Tel Aviv University in 2000. For eight and a half years I worked at the Rabin Medical Center (Beilinson), in the inpatient wards, the outpatient institute and the hospital gym, and then for three years at the Pelvic Floor Unit of the Sheba Medical Center (Tel HaShomer). Since then I have been working at my clinics in Herzliya and Tel Aviv.

Over the years I came to understand that the pelvic floor does not stand on its own: it is connected to the breath, the abdomen, the back, the hips and the connective tissues that wrap around all of them.

This understanding led me to study the world of fascia, and from there to develop the PFFM method, Pelvic Floor Fascial Mobilization: internal manual treatment of the fascial tissues of the pelvic floor. I have published two articles on the method in the scientific Journal of Bodywork and Movement Therapies, and it was at the center of my master's research. I lecture in Hebrew and in English, at professional conferences in Israel and abroad and to the general public.

Fascial Net Plastination Project

In June 2018 and January 2020 I was a member of the dissection team of the Fascial Net Plastination Project at the Plastinarium in Guben, Germany: an international anatomical research project founded in 2018 on the initiative of Dr. Robert Schleip and co-led with Prof. Carla Stecco, together with the Fascia Research Society and Body Worlds. The project's aim was to show, for the first time, the fascial network of the human body using the plastination method developed by Dr. Gunther von Hagens.

As part of the project I worked on the cross-section specimen of the abdominal fascia, one of the specimens shown in the exhibition "Fascia in a New Light" at the Fascia Research Congress in Berlin (2018). As I told the project team at the time: "It was a dream come true, to switch on the light in the area I touch almost every day in complete darkness." The project's high point was the unveiling of FR:EIA (2021): the world's first plastinate of the fascial network of a whole body, now on display at the Body Worlds museum in Berlin.

Accompanying mothers through cesarean birth (2017 to 2024)

In 2014, at a conference given by Luc Guillarme, I was introduced to the French FAUCS cesarean technique and to the use of a blowing device during the operation, which lets the mother produce the pushing force to deliver the baby herself. I later took part in bringing the method to Israel, and between 2017 and 2024 I accompanied mothers throughout the whole operation, in a role I created and shaped: abdominal birth coordinator. The model was later adopted in France as well.

The support was built as a complete model, in three parts:

  1. 01

    Preparation in advance: a Zoom meeting with the mother, and detailed instruction videos on the course of the operation and the sensations to expect at each stage.

  2. 02

    Support in real time: guidance in using the blowing device, explaining what is happening at every moment, and being at the woman's side from the beginning of the operation to the end.

  3. 03

    Bonding in the operating room: from the moment of birth I held the baby on the mother's chest, while the operation was still under way, and made sure of skin-to-skin contact, a first breastfeed and the baby's first meeting with its parents.

One part of the model, which I am especially proud of, was meant for mothers who had to undergo the operation under general anesthesia: preparing the father in advance for what to expect, accompanying him throughout the operation, and making sure that even then the baby was placed on its mother's chest as soon as it was born.

I am a co-author of an article on the safety of the method and the mothers' recovery, published in 2024 in the Journal of Obstetrics and Gynaecology Canada.
